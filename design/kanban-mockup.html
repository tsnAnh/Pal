Card states

The column already says the state, so the card drops the state chip, the Contract chip and the age. A card speaks only when it has something to add: a live dot while running, the reason while blocked, a quieter title when done.

Pull request chip

One capsule, glyph plus number, colour carries the state. Draft is an outline because it is not yet a claim.

Shape experiment

VerdictCapsule with the number; the glyph changes per state so colour is never the only cue; a blocked card states the reason on its own line. The square badge read as a button; the bare glyph lost the number, which is what people scan for.

Assignee avatar

The real Pal avatar, drawn from the same rule as ClassicDrawing: shape and hue come from the bot name hash, eyes pick light or dark ink by body luminance.

Placement experiment

Verdict20 pt avatar with the bot name, bottom-leading. 16 pt loses the eyes; 24 pt competes with the title; a top-right avatar pushed long titles into a ragged right edge.

Glass levels

Glass sits on the navigation layer only: toolbar, columns, controls. Cards are content, so they get a near-solid fill with the same specular edge and no blur of their own.

VerdictBlur 28 px, saturate 1.6, tint 52% dark / 56% light. Below 16 px the colour field shows as blotches behind text; above 40 px the column edge turns to fog.

Column density

Comfortable shows three title lines at 268 px; compact shows two at 248 px. Both fit five columns at 1440 px without clipping.

VerdictComfortable by default. Real chief titles run 90 to 140 characters; two lines cut most of them before the verb that says what the work is.

Palette

Tinted keeps the tokens and adds a soft colour field behind the glass. Graphite is the tokens alone. Black uses PalPalette.derivedBlack. Switch them in the controls, bottom right.

RecommendTinted. Graphite is correct but flat, which is the complaint today; Black loses the glass because there is nothing behind it to refract.

Card detail

Opens in the 360 pt inspector on Mac and as a sheet on iPhone. The body is readable prose, not a key dump.
